In short

Bond Time helps you put your phone down for a set time by covering other apps with a full-screen block. Phone, Messages and Camera stay available.

The app is free, with no ads or purchases, and works offline. It is a wellbeing tool, not a safety or parental-control system.

2. What the app does

You choose a duration and start a session. While it runs, Bond Time covers apps other than Phone, Messages and Camera with a full-screen block. Stopping a session early has a five-minute wind-down. You can save duration presets and use a home-screen widget, and a session carries on after your phone restarts.

3. Price

Bond Time is free. It has no ads, no in-app purchases and no subscriptions.

4. The Accessibility service

Bond Time needs you to turn on its Accessibility service. It uses the service only to see which app is in front and to show the block; it does not read the content of your screen. You can turn the service off at any time in your device's Accessibility settings, which stops Bond Time from blocking apps. See the Bond Time Privacy Policy for details.

5. Emergencies and messages

Phone stays available during a session, but do not rely on Bond Time in an emergency: use your phone's emergency call feature. Notifications and messages from blocked apps may be missed or delayed until the session ends, so do not start a session when you need to respond to other apps.

6. Limits of blocking

Blocking depends on your device, its version of Android, the maker's settings and battery-saving features. Bond Time may not block every app or screen on every device, and it is not a security or anti-tamper tool.

7. Using Bond Time with others
